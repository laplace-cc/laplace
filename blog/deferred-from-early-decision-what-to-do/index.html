Deferred From Early Decision or Early Action? What to Do Next

Being deferred is disappointing, but it isn't a no. A deferral means the college wants to see your application again alongside the regular pool. Thousands of students are admitted after a deferral every year. What you do in the next few weeks can matter.

First: read the letter carefully

Colleges handle deferrals differently. Some invite updates; some ask you not to send anything beyond mid-year grades. Follow the instructions exactly. Ignoring a "please don't send additional materials" request doesn't help.

If you were deferred from Early Decision

A deferral releases you from the binding commitment. You're free to apply Early Decision II elsewhere, and that's worth considering seriously if you have another school you'd happily commit to. See our guide to early application rounds.

Write a letter of continued interest (if allowed)

A letter of continued interest (LOCI) is a short, specific note to the admissions office. A strong one:

  1. Confirms the school is still a top choice (and says you'll attend if admitted, only if that's true).
  2. Shares meaningful updates since you applied: new results, leadership, research, a strong semester.
  3. Adds one or two specific reasons you fit that weren't in your original application.
  4. Stays under a page. Admissions officers read a lot of these.

Send it to your regional admissions officer if you know who that is, or through the applicant portal.

What else to do

  • Keep your grades up. Mid-year senior grades are often the most important new information a college sees.
  • Send real updates only. A new award or major project helps; a minor club role doesn't need a separate email.
  • Don't flood the inbox. One thoughtful letter, plus updates when something significant happens, is plenty.
  • Finish your other applications strong. Regular Decision deadlines arrive fast. Use our regular decision checklist.

Rethink your list

A deferral is useful information. If you were deferred from several reach schools, it may be time to add a few more target schools to your regular round. Our guide on building a balanced college list can help you rebalance quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the chances of getting in after a deferral?

It varies widely by school and year. Some selective colleges admit only a small share of deferred applicants, while others admit many. A deferral means you're still under consideration in the regular round.

Should I send a letter of continued interest after being deferred?

If the college allows updates, yes. Keep it under a page, confirm your interest, share meaningful updates since you applied, and follow any instructions in your deferral letter.

Can I apply ED II if I was deferred from ED I?

Yes. A deferral releases you from the binding ED I commitment, so you may apply Early Decision II at another college.
