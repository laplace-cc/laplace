1f:T819,
<p>For Georgia families, the HOPE and Zell Miller scholarships are some of the most valuable financial aid available, and they're merit-based, not need-based. Understanding the requirements early, ideally by freshman year, can save a family tens of thousands of dollars.</p>

<h2>The quick comparison</h2>
<table>
<tr><th></th><th>HOPE Scholarship</th><th>Zell Miller Scholarship</th></tr>
<tr><td>High school GPA</td><td>3.0 in core academic courses</td><td>3.7 in core academic courses</td></tr>
<tr><td>Test score</td><td>Not required</td><td>1200 SAT (Math + EBRW) or 25 ACT, in a single sitting</td></tr>
<tr><td>Rigor courses</td><td>Required</td><td>Required</td></tr>
<tr><td>Award at public colleges</td><td>A large share of tuition</td><td>Full tuition</td></tr>
<tr><td>GPA to keep it in college</td><td>3.0 at checkpoints</td><td>3.3 at checkpoints</td></tr>
</table>
<p>The Zell Miller ACT requirement was lowered from 26 to 25 for students graduating more recently, and rules do change, so always confirm current requirements on <a href="https://www.gafutures.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GAfutures</a>, the official source.</p>

<h2>How the HOPE GPA is calculated</h2>
<p>The HOPE GPA is not your school's GPA. The Georgia Student Finance Commission recalculates it using only core academic courses: English, math, science, social studies, and foreign language. Electives don't count. AP, IB, and dual enrollment courses can receive additional weight under the state's rules. Students can view their HOPE GPA on GAfutures, and we recommend checking it every year, not just senior year.</p>

<h2>Rigor course requirement</h2>
<p>Both scholarships require students to complete a minimum number of designated rigor courses, such as advanced math, advanced science, AP and IB courses, dual enrollment, and certain foreign language courses. Plan these into the four-year schedule early; it's much harder to catch up in senior year. Georgia's <a href="/blog/georgia-dual-enrollment-guide/">Dual Enrollment program</a> is one way to earn rigor credit.</p>

<h2>Automatic Zell Miller eligibility</h2>
<p>Valedictorians and salutatorians are generally Zell Miller eligible regardless of GPA and test score thresholds, though other requirements still apply. Check GAfutures for the exact conditions.</p>

<h2>Keeping the scholarship in college</h2>
<p>Eligibility is re-checked at set credit-hour checkpoints and at the end of each spring term. Zell Miller recipients need to maintain a 3.3 college GPA; a student who drops below that but keeps a 3.0 may move to HOPE. There are also limits on total attempted hours. The practical takeaway: the first year of college matters a lot, so choose a manageable first-semester schedule.</p>

<h2>How this affects your college list</h2>
<p>For a Zell-eligible student, in-state public options like <a href="/blog/how-to-get-into-georgia-tech/">Georgia Tech</a> and <a href="/blog/how-to-get-into-uga/">UGA</a> can be extraordinarily affordable. That should be part of every Georgia family's list-building conversation, especially when comparing against private colleges and out-of-state options. HOPE and Zell can also be used at eligible private colleges in Georgia, though the award amount differs.</p>
