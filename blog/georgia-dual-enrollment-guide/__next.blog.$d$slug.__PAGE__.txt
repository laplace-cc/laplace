<p>Georgia's Dual Enrollment (DE) program lets high school students take college courses for both high school and college credit. It's a powerful option, and it's also one of the most common decisions Georgia families ask us about: should my student take DE, AP, or both?</p>

<h2>How the program works</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Who:</strong> eligible Georgia students, typically in grades 10–12, who are admitted by a participating college. Eligibility rules for 10th graders are more limited, especially at four-year institutions.</li>
<li><strong>Funding cap:</strong> state funding covers up to 30 semester hours of eligible coursework in total.</li>
<li><strong>Per-term limits</strong> apply on how many hours are funded each term.</li>
<li><strong>Where:</strong> participating colleges include University System of Georgia institutions, technical colleges, and some private colleges, either on campus, online, or at the high school.</li>
</ul>
<p>Rules are set by the Georgia Student Finance Commission and change periodically, so confirm details on <a href="https://www.gafutures.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GAfutures</a> and with your school counselor.</p>

<h2>DE vs. AP: how selective colleges see it</h2>
<p>Both show rigor. The differences are in how credit transfers and how the course is perceived:</p>
<table>
<tr><th></th><th>AP</th><th>Dual Enrollment</th></tr>
<tr><td>Standardization</td><td>National exam, easy for any college to interpret</td><td>Varies by college and instructor</td></tr>
<tr><td>Credit</td><td>Depends on exam score and the receiving college</td><td>A real college transcript; transfers well within Georgia public colleges, varies elsewhere</td></tr>
<tr><td>Best for</td><td>Core subjects where your school offers the AP</td><td>Subjects beyond your school's offerings, or students who've exhausted the AP sequence</td></tr>
</table>
<p>For highly selective private colleges, AP and IB courses in core subjects are often the clearest signal, while DE shines when it lets a student go beyond what their high school offers, for example multivariable calculus or linear algebra after AP Calculus BC.</p>

<h2>When DE is the right call</h2>
<ul>
<li>Your high school has run out of advanced courses in a subject you care about.</li>
<li>You're aiming at Georgia public universities, where DE credit transfers predictably.</li>
<li>You want to save on tuition by entering college with credits.</li>
<li>You need rigor courses toward <a href="/blog/hope-vs-zell-miller-scholarship/">HOPE or Zell Miller</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Watch-outs</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>DE grades go on a permanent college transcript.</strong> A weak DE grade follows you, including into future graduate or professional school applications.</li>
<li><strong>Using up the 30-hour cap</strong> on courses that won't matter later is a waste. Plan which courses carry the most value.</li>
<li><strong>Schedule logistics</strong> can crowd out activities. Make sure DE doesn't eliminate the extracurricular depth colleges also look for.</li>
</ul>
<p>The right mix depends on your student's school, goals, and target colleges.
