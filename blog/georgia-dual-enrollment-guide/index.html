Georgia Dual Enrollment: Is It Better Than AP?

Georgia's Dual Enrollment (DE) program lets high school students take college courses for both high school and college credit. It's a powerful option, and it's also one of the most common decisions Georgia families ask us about: should my student take DE, AP, or both?

How the program works

  • Who: eligible Georgia students, typically in grades 10–12, who are admitted by a participating college. Eligibility rules for 10th graders are more limited, especially at four-year institutions.
  • Funding cap: state funding covers up to 30 semester hours of eligible coursework in total.
  • Per-term limits apply on how many hours are funded each term.
  • Where: participating colleges include University System of Georgia institutions, technical colleges, and some private colleges, either on campus, online, or at the high school.

Rules are set by the Georgia Student Finance Commission and change periodically, so confirm details on GAfutures and with your school counselor.

DE vs. AP: how selective colleges see it

Both show rigor. The differences are in how credit transfers and how the course is perceived:

APDual Enrollment
StandardizationNational exam, easy for any college to interpretVaries by college and instructor
CreditDepends on exam score and the receiving collegeA real college transcript; transfers well within Georgia public colleges, varies elsewhere
Best forCore subjects where your school offers the APSubjects beyond your school's offerings, or students who've exhausted the AP sequence

For highly selective private colleges, AP and IB courses in core subjects are often the clearest signal, while DE shines when it lets a student go beyond what their high school offers, for example multivariable calculus or linear algebra after AP Calculus BC.

When DE is the right call

  • Your high school has run out of advanced courses in a subject you care about.
  • You're aiming at Georgia public universities, where DE credit transfers predictably.
  • You want to save on tuition by entering college with credits.
  • You need rigor courses toward HOPE or Zell Miller.

Watch-outs

  • DE grades go on a permanent college transcript. A weak DE grade follows you, including into future graduate or professional school applications.
  • Using up the 30-hour cap on courses that won't matter later is a waste. Plan which courses carry the most value.
  • Schedule logistics can crowd out activities. Make sure DE doesn't eliminate the extracurricular depth colleges also look for.

The right mix depends on your student's school, goals, and target colleges. See how rigor plays into Georgia Tech and UGA admissions, and our guide on how many AP classes to take.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is dual enrollment free in Georgia?

Eligible courses are funded by the state up to a 30 semester-hour cap. Costs beyond that cap, or for ineligible courses, may fall to the family. Check current rules on GAfutures.

Do colleges prefer AP or dual enrollment?

Both demonstrate rigor. Highly selective colleges often find AP and IB easiest to compare across applicants, while dual enrollment is most valuable when it lets a student go beyond what their high school offers.

Can 9th graders take dual enrollment in Georgia?

Georgia's program is generally for students in grades 10–12, with more limited options for 10th graders. Confirm eligibility with your counselor and the participating college.
