<p>Emory is Atlanta's most selective private university and one of the most strategic applications on many students' lists. It has two binding Early Decision rounds and two separate entry points, Emory College of Arts and Sciences in Atlanta and Oxford College in Oxford, Georgia. Used well, those options can meaningfully improve a student's chances.</p>

<h2>ED I, ED II, and Regular Decision</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Early Decision I:</strong> deadline November 1, decisions by mid-December. Binding.</li>
<li><strong>Early Decision II:</strong> deadline January 1. Also binding. A good option for students who were deferred or denied elsewhere in the early round, or whose first choice became clear later.</li>
<li><strong>Regular Decision:</strong> January deadline, non-binding.</li>
</ul>
<p>Confirm current dates and financial aid deadlines on Emory's official <a href="https://apply.emory.edu/apply/first-year/plans-deadlines/early-decision.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Early Decision page</a>. If you're weighing ED anywhere, read our guide to <a href="/blog/early-decision-vs-early-action/">Early Decision vs. Early Action</a> first, and run the net price calculator before committing.</p>

<h2>Emory College vs. Oxford College</h2>
<p>Oxford College is a two-year, small, residential campus about 40 miles from Atlanta. Students spend their first two years at Oxford and then continue to Emory's Atlanta campus to finish their degree. Students can apply to Emory College, Oxford College, or both.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Applying to both</strong> gives two chances at an Emory degree. If admitted to both, the student chooses which to attend.</li>
<li><strong>Oxford suits</strong> students who want a small liberal-arts experience with close faculty contact before moving to a research university.</li>
<li>Read Emory's rules carefully on combining Oxford and Emory College in the ED rounds, because they affect which applications are binding.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What Emory looks for</h2>
<p>Emory reads holistically, but a few themes show up consistently in strong applications:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Intellectual curiosity</strong> that goes beyond grades: independent reading, research, projects, and questions the student is still chasing.</li>
<li><strong>Community engagement</strong> — Emory values students who will contribute to campus life, not just pass through it.</li>
<li><strong>Clear academic interests</strong>, especially for students drawn to Emory's strengths in health sciences, public health, business, and the humanities.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The supplements</h2>
<p>Emory's short supplemental questions change from year to year and are deliberately brief. Brevity is the challenge: every sentence has to carry information about the student. Specific, personal answers outperform polished generalities. Our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-write-supplemental-essays/">writing supplemental essays</a> covers the approach.</p>

<h2>Pre-med and public health applicants</h2>
<p>Emory is a frequent choice for students interested in medicine and public health because of its hospital system and the Rollins School of Public Health. If that's your student, make sure the application shows genuine engagement with health, such as research, clinical exposure, or community health work, rather than just stating an interest. Our guide to <a href="/blog/high-school-research-experience/">getting research experience in high school</a> is a good starting point.</p>
