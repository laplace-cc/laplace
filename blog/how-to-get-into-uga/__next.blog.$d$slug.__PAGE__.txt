<p>The University of Georgia has become dramatically more competitive over the last decade, and application numbers keep climbing. For many Georgia families, UGA is the anchor of the college list, which makes it worth understanding exactly how the review works rather than relying on what admitted students looked like five years ago.</p>

<h2>Key dates</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Early Action:</strong> application due October 15, with supporting materials due a couple of weeks later.</li>
<li><strong>Regular Decision:</strong> application due January 1, materials due mid-January.</li>
<li><strong>Commitment deposit:</strong> May 1.</li>
</ul>
<p>These dates come from UGA's official counselor materials for the current cycle; always double-check the <a href="https://www.admissions.uga.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UGA admissions site</a>. UGA's Early Action is non-binding. Early applicants receive one of three answers: admit, deny, or defer to Regular Decision.</p>

<h2>Rigor is the center of the review</h2>
<p>UGA has been unusually transparent that the academic record, and especially the rigor of the courses a student took, carries the most weight. That means:</p>
<ul>
<li>Choosing AP, IB, or dual enrollment courses over easier options matters, as long as grades stay strong.</li>
<li>UGA recalculates GPA using core academic courses, so electives won't inflate the number they see.</li>
<li>Senior-year courses count. Dropping rigor senior year is one of the most common unforced errors we see.</li>
</ul>

<h2>When the essay and activities matter most</h2>
<p>For students whose academic record clearly places them in or out, the rest of the application matters less. For the large group of applicants in the middle, the essay, activities, and recommendations do real work. That's why it's a mistake to treat the UGA essay as an afterthought: for many Georgia students it's the tiebreaker.</p>

<h2>Writing the UGA essay</h2>
<p>UGA's short-answer prompt changes over time, but the goal is consistent: show who you are beyond the transcript, in your own voice. Effective responses tend to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Focus on one specific moment, habit, or idea rather than summarizing your life.</li>
<li>Show reflection — what you noticed, how you changed, what you'd do differently.</li>
<li>Avoid repeating the activities list. Use the space for something the reader can't learn elsewhere.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you're also writing a Common App personal statement, our <a href="/blog/how-to-write-common-app-essay/">Common App essay guide</a> covers how to make the two pieces complement each other.</p>

<h2>Activities: depth over volume</h2>
<p>Like most selective schools, UGA values sustained commitment and impact more than a long list. Long-term involvement, leadership that changed something, jobs, and family responsibilities all count. Our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-write-common-app-activities-list/">writing the activities list</a> shows how to describe them well in limited space.</p>

<h2>Honors College and scholarships</h2>
<p>UGA's Honors College has its own considerations, and Georgia residents should understand how the <a href="/blog/hope-vs-zell-miller-scholarship/">HOPE and Zell Miller scholarships</a> apply at UGA. For many families, a Zell-eligible UGA admit is one of the best values in the country.</p>

<h2>Building a Georgia college list around UGA</h2>
<p>Because UGA is competitive and its admissions are heavily academic, it should rarely be the only Georgia public on a list. Pair it with <a href="/blog/how-to-get-into-georgia-tech/">Georgia Tech</a> if the student is STEM-focused, and with other University System of Georgia schools that fit your student's goals. Our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-college-list/">building a balanced college list</a> explains how.</p>
