<p>The Georgia Governor's Honors Program (GHP) is a selective residential summer program for academically and artistically talented Georgia high school students. It's state-funded, it's highly regarded by Georgia colleges, and it's a genuinely great experience. It's also easy to miss, because everything starts with a nomination at your school, often months before families start thinking about summer.</p>

<h2>How GHP works</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Who:</strong> Georgia high school students in the eligible grades, nominated through a Georgia public school district, independent school, or state charter school. Grade eligibility is set each year by the program, so check the current handbook.</li>
<li><strong>What:</strong> a multi-week summer residential program focused on a single major area, from math, science, and engineering to fine arts, communicative arts, social studies, and world languages.</li>
<li><strong>Once:</strong> students can attend GHP only once.</li>
</ul>
<p>The program is administered by the Governor's Office of Student Achievement (GOSA). The official <a href="https://gosa.georgia.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GOSA site</a> publishes the coordinator handbook and timeline each year.</p>

<h2>The process, step by step</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Local nomination (late summer / early fall).</strong> Teachers nominate students in a subject area, often in a short window in August or September. Tell your teachers and your school's GHP coordinator early that you're interested.</li>
<li><strong>Local selection.</strong> Your district or school runs its own process, which may include an application, a portfolio or audition, and an interview. Only a limited number of nominees advance from each school or district.</li>
<li><strong>State application (winter).</strong> Students who advance complete the state application. Incomplete applications aren't considered.</li>
<li><strong>State interviews and auditions (early spring).</strong> Finalists interview or audition in their subject area.</li>
<li><strong>Decisions (spring)</strong> and the program itself in June and July.</li>
</ol>

<h2>How to stand out</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pick your subject strategically.</strong> Choose the area where you have the most depth and evidence, not just the highest grade.</li>
<li><strong>Bring real work.</strong> Projects, research, writing, compositions, or competition results in your area make the application and interview concrete.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare for the interview like a conversation.</strong> Interviewers want to see curiosity and how you think. Practice explaining your work and the questions it raised, out loud.</li>
<li><strong>Get your teacher on board early.</strong> A teacher who knows your work well can write a stronger nomination.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why it matters for college</h2>
<p>GHP is well known to admissions officers at Georgia colleges and is a meaningful honor on any application. More importantly, the summer itself often sparks the kind of focused interest that shapes a strong application narrative. It pairs well with the other opportunities in our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-build-extracurriculars/">building extracurriculars</a>.</p>
