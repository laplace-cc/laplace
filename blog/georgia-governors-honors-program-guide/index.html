Georgia Governor's Honors Program (GHP): Nomination, Interview, and How to Stand Out

The Georgia Governor's Honors Program (GHP) is a selective residential summer program for academically and artistically talented Georgia high school students. It's state-funded, it's highly regarded by Georgia colleges, and it's a genuinely great experience. It's also easy to miss, because everything starts with a nomination at your school, often months before families start thinking about summer.

How GHP works

  • Who: Georgia high school students in the eligible grades, nominated through a Georgia public school district, independent school, or state charter school. Grade eligibility is set each year by the program, so check the current handbook.
  • What: a multi-week summer residential program focused on a single major area, from math, science, and engineering to fine arts, communicative arts, social studies, and world languages.
  • Once: students can attend GHP only once.

The program is administered by the Governor's Office of Student Achievement (GOSA). The official GOSA site publishes the coordinator handbook and timeline each year.

The process, step by step

  1. Local nomination (late summer / early fall). Teachers nominate students in a subject area, often in a short window in August or September. Tell your teachers and your school's GHP coordinator early that you're interested.
  2. Local selection. Your district or school runs its own process, which may include an application, a portfolio or audition, and an interview. Only a limited number of nominees advance from each school or district.
  3. State application (winter). Students who advance complete the state application. Incomplete applications aren't considered.
  4. State interviews and auditions (early spring). Finalists interview or audition in their subject area.
  5. Decisions (spring) and the program itself in June and July.

How to stand out

  • Pick your subject strategically. Choose the area where you have the most depth and evidence, not just the highest grade.
  • Bring real work. Projects, research, writing, compositions, or competition results in your area make the application and interview concrete.
  • Prepare for the interview like a conversation. Interviewers want to see curiosity and how you think. Practice explaining your work and the questions it raised, out loud.
  • Get your teacher on board early. A teacher who knows your work well can write a stronger nomination.

Why it matters for college

GHP is well known to admissions officers at Georgia colleges and is a meaningful honor on any application. More importantly, the summer itself often sparks the kind of focused interest that shapes a strong application narrative. Frequently Asked Questions

Can you apply to GHP without a nomination?

No. Students must be nominated through a Georgia public school district, independent school, or state charter school. Ask your teachers and your school's GHP coordinator about the local nomination window.

When are GHP nominations?

Local nominations usually happen in late summer or early fall, and the state application follows in winter. Exact dates vary by district, so check with your school's coordinator.

Is GHP free?

GHP is a state-funded program. Check the current GOSA handbook for any costs families may be responsible for.
