<h2>Writing descriptions that show impact</h2>
<p>With 150 characters, every word has to carry information. A simple formula: <strong>action verb + what you did + result or scale.</strong></p>
<table>
<tr><th>Weak</th><th>Strong</th></tr>
<tr><td>Member of the robotics club. Helped build robots and went to competitions.</td><td>Designed drivetrain for 40-lb competition robot; led 6-person build team; reached state semifinals after rebuilding auto routine.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Volunteer at the library. Helped kids with reading.</td><td>Created weekly reading program for 15 elementary students; trained 4 new volunteers; program now runs year-round.</td></tr>
</table>
<p>(Examples are illustrative.) Use numbers where they're honest, cut filler like "I was responsible for," and use the position field for your title so the description can focus on what you did.</p>

<h2>Things students forget to include</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Jobs</strong>, paid or unpaid. Colleges value work experience.</li>
<li><strong>Family responsibilities</strong>, such as caring for siblings or relatives.</li>
<li><strong>Independent projects</strong>: an app, a business, research, writing, a YouTube channel.</li>
<li><strong>Summer programs</strong> and courses taken outside school.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Exaggerating hours or titles. Your counselor and recommenders may describe the same activity.</li>
<li>Wasting space repeating the organization name in the description.</li>
<li>Listing ten shallow activities instead of a few deep ones. See our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-build-extracurriculars/">building extracurriculars</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>Your activities list should connect to your essays. Read our <a href="/blog/how-to-write-common-app-essay/">Common App essay guide</a> to make the pieces work together.</p>
