How to Choose a College Admissions Consultant: 10 Questions to Ask

College consulting is unregulated, so anyone can call themselves a consultant. That makes choosing one harder than it should be. These questions will tell you most of what you need to know.

10 questions to ask

  1. Who exactly will work with my student? Get a name and background, not just the firm's reputation.
  2. What's your own admissions experience? Recent, firsthand experience with the schools your student is targeting is valuable.
  3. What's included, in writing? Essays, list, activities, roadmap, interview prep, number of live hours.
  4. How fast do you respond? In the fall, a week-long turnaround on an essay draft can be a problem.
  5. How do you handle essays? The answer should be coaching and feedback. If they offer to write essays, walk away.
  6. Can I see examples of your work? Sample roadmaps, anonymized feedback, or results with context.
  7. How do you build a college list? Look for balance and fit, not just prestige.
  8. What happens if we're unhappy? Clear terms on missed deliverables protect both sides.
  9. What does it cost, and can it be scoped to what we need? See our guide to consultant pricing.
  10. What do you need from us? A good consultant expects an engaged student and is clear about it.

Red flags

  • Guarantees of admission to specific schools.
  • Offers to write essays or fabricate activities.
  • High-pressure sales or vague deliverables.
  • A salesperson who disappears after you sign.

What a good engagement looks like

You should leave the first call with a clear sense of your student's strengths and gaps, a realistic view of their options, and a concrete plan, whether or not you hire the consultant. That's the standard we hold ourselves to. Compare approaches in Laplace vs. traditional consultants.

Frequently Asked Questions

What qualifications should a college consultant have?

There's no required license. Look for direct, recent experience with the admissions process and schools your student is targeting, clear references or examples of work, and a transparent, written scope.

Should a college consultant write my essays?

No. Essays must be the student's own work. A consultant should brainstorm, give feedback, and help revise, never write the essay.
