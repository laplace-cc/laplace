<p>College consulting is unregulated, so anyone can call themselves a consultant. That makes choosing one harder than it should be. These questions will tell you most of what you need to know.</p>

<h2>10 questions to ask</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Who exactly will work with my student?</strong> Get a name and background, not just the firm's reputation.</li>
<li><strong>What's your own admissions experience?</strong> Recent, firsthand experience with the schools your student is targeting is valuable.</li>
<li><strong>What's included, in writing?</strong> Essays, list, activities, roadmap, interview prep, number of live hours.</li>
<li><strong>How fast do you respond?</strong> In the fall, a week-long turnaround on an essay draft can be a problem.</li>
<li><strong>How do you handle essays?</strong> The answer should be coaching and feedback. If they offer to write essays, walk away.</li>
<li><strong>Can I see examples of your work?</strong> Sample roadmaps, anonymized feedback, or results with context.</li>
<li><strong>How do you build a college list?</strong> Look for balance and fit, not just prestige.</li>
<li><strong>What happens if we're unhappy?</strong> Clear terms on missed deliverables protect both sides.</li>
<li><strong>What does it cost, and can it be scoped to what we need?</strong> See our guide to <a href="/blog/how-much-does-college-consulting-cost/">consultant pricing</a>.</li>
<li><strong>What do you need from us?</strong> A good consultant expects an engaged student and is clear about it.</li>
</ol>
