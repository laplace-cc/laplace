<p>A waitlist means the college would like to admit you if space opens up. How many students come off the waitlist varies enormously from year to year and school to school. You can't control those numbers, but you can control how you respond.</p>

<h2>1. Opt in right away</h2>
<p>Most colleges require you to accept a spot on the waitlist through the applicant portal. If you don't opt in, you won't be considered.</p>

<h2>2. Deposit somewhere else</h2>
<p>Waitlist movement often happens after May 1. Accept an offer and pay a deposit at another college you'd be happy to attend, so you have a guaranteed place.</p>

<h2>3. Send a strong update letter</h2>
<p>If the college accepts updates, send a short letter of continued interest. Confirm that the school is a top choice (say you'll attend if admitted only if it's true), share significant updates since you applied, and add one or two specific reasons you fit. Our <a href="/blog/deferred-from-early-decision-what-to-do/">deferral guide</a> walks through the same letter structure.</p>
