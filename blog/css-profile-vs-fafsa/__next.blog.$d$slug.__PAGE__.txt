<p>Financial aid paperwork is one of the most stressful parts of the college process, and the two main forms, the FAFSA and the CSS Profile, are easy to confuse. Getting them right, on time, can make a big difference in what your family pays.</p>

<h2>The FAFSA</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>What it is:</strong> the Free Application for Federal Student Aid, run by the U.S. Department of Education.</li>
<li><strong>Who uses it:</strong> nearly every college, for federal grants, loans, and work-study; many states and colleges also use it for their own aid.</li>
<li><strong>Cost:</strong> free.</li>
<li><strong>When:</strong> typically opens October 1 for the following school year. File as early as you can.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The CSS Profile</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>What it is:</strong> a form run by the College Board that colleges use to award their own institutional aid.</li>
<li><strong>Who uses it:</strong> hundreds of colleges, mostly private, including many highly selective schools.</li>
<li><strong>Cost:</strong> there is a fee per school, with fee waivers available for eligible families.</li>
<li><strong>What's different:</strong> it asks for more detail than the FAFSA and some schools consider things like home equity or a non-custodial parent's finances.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key differences</h2>
<table>
<tr><th></th><th>FAFSA</th><th>CSS Profile</th></tr>
<tr><td>Aid it unlocks</td><td>Federal and often state aid</td><td>Institutional aid at participating colleges</td></tr>

<tr><td>Depth</td><td>Simpler</td><td>More detailed</td></tr>
<tr><td>Deadlines</td><td>Federal deadline is late, but colleges and states set earlier priority deadlines</td><td>Set by each college, often aligned with ED/EA/RD</td></tr>

<h2>Deadlines matter more than people think</h2>
<p>If you apply Early Decision or Early Action, many colleges want financial aid forms by a deadline in November or December. Missing an aid deadline can reduce or delay your package. Make a list of every school's aid deadline when you finalize your <a href="/blog/how-to-make-a-college-list/">college list</a>.</p>

<h2>Run net price calculators first</h2>
<p>Every college has a net price calculator on its website. Run it before deciding where to apply, and especially before applying <a href="/blog/early-decision-vs-early-action/">Early Decision</a>. Georgia families should also factor in <a href="/blog/hope-vs-zell-miller-scholarship/">HOPE and Zell Miller</a>, which can make in-state options very affordable.</p>
