Is College Consulting Worth It? An Honest Answer

We run a consulting firm, so take this with that in mind, but we'll be straight: college consulting isn't the right investment for every family. Here's how to think about whether it's right for yours.

What a consultant can't change

No consultant can rewrite a transcript, invent achievements, or guarantee an admit. Grades, course rigor, and years of genuine involvement are the foundation of every application, and they belong to the student.

What a good consultant can change

  • Strategy. Which schools, which round, which major. Choosing Early Decision well or building a balanced list can change outcomes more than any single essay.
  • The story. Most strong students under-sell themselves. A good consultant helps them find the thread that connects their activities and write about it clearly.
  • Time. Earlier help means better course choices, better summers, and real projects instead of last-minute padding.
  • Execution. Deadlines, documents, recommendations, and the dozens of supplements that pile up in the fall.

Who benefits most

  • Students at schools where counselors manage hundreds of students and can't give individual attention.
  • First-generation applicants navigating an unfamiliar process.
  • Students aiming at highly selective schools, where small differences in presentation matter.
  • Students with unusual profiles (research, startups, competitions, international backgrounds) that need careful framing.
  • Families who want a plan early, in 9th or 10th grade, rather than a scramble in senior fall.

Who might not need one

  • Students with a strong, engaged school counselor and a straightforward list.
  • Students who are highly self-directed and have access to good free resources.
  • Families who would be stretched financially. A consultant should never be a financial strain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do college consultants really help you get in?

They can't guarantee admission, but good consultants improve strategy, school selection, essays, and timing, which can meaningfully change outcomes, especially at highly selective schools.

When should you hire a college consultant?

Earlier is usually better. Starting in 9th or 10th grade allows help with courses, summers, and activities, but many families start in junior year or the summer before senior year for applications and essays.

Is a college consultant better than a school counselor?

They serve different roles. School counselors write recommendations and know your school well, but often manage hundreds of students. A consultant provides individual, in-depth support.
