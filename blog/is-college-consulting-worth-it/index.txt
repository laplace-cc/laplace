<p>We run a consulting firm, so take this with that in mind, but we'll be straight: college consulting isn't the right investment for every family. Here's how to think about whether it's right for yours.</p>

<h2>What a consultant can't change</h2>
<p>No consultant can rewrite a transcript, invent achievements, or guarantee an admit. Grades, course rigor, and years of genuine involvement are the foundation of every application, and they belong to the student.</p>

<h2>What a good consultant can change</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Strategy.</strong> Which schools, which round, which major. Choosing Early Decision well or building a balanced list can change outcomes more than any single essay.</li>
<li><strong>The story.</strong> Most strong students under-sell themselves. A good consultant helps them find the thread that connects their activities and write about it clearly.</li>
<li><strong>Time.</strong> Earlier help means better course choices, better summers, and real projects instead of last-minute padding.</li>
<li><strong>Execution.</strong> Deadlines, documents, recommendations, and the dozens of supplements that pile up in the fall.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Who benefits most</h2>
<ul>
<li>Students at schools where counselors manage hundreds of students and can't give individual attention.</li>
<li>First-generation applicants navigating an unfamiliar process.</li>
<li>Students aiming at highly selective schools, where small differences in presentation matter.</li>
<li>Students with unusual profiles (research, startups, competitions, international backgrounds) that need careful framing.</li>
<li>Families who want a plan early, in 9th or 10th grade, rather than a scramble in senior fall.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Who might not need one</h2>
<ul>
<li>Students with a strong, engaged school counselor and a straightforward list.</li>
<li>Students who are highly self-directed and have access to good free resources.</li>
<li>Families who would be stretched financially. A consultant should never be a financial strain.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Questions to ask before deciding</h2>
<p>Read our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-choose-a-college-admissions-consultant/">how to choose a college admissions consultant</a> and our breakdown of <a href="/blog/how-much-does-college-consulting-cost/">what consulting costs</a>. A good firm will tell you honestly if they don't think they can help.</p>
